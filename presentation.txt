I implemented two first order gradient descent methods for both functions in the first row of our assignment. Both methods use the negative analytical gradient. The first method reduces the step until the sufficient decrease condition holds. The second method uses my own Golden Section search on the lecture interval between zero and one. I saved every iterate and counted every function and gradient evaluation. For the exponential function, the methods needed 10 and 5 updates. For the cosine function, they needed 33 and 39. Both functions are strongly convex and have a unique global minimum. I checked the results against independent reference solutions. The contour figures show the paths and search directions. The gradient is perpendicular to a level curve. An exact interior line minimum also makes successive gradient directions perpendicular. With approximate or boundary line searches, that equality need not hold exactly. The results show why iteration counts must be compared together with evaluation counts and final accuracy.
